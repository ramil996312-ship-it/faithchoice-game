## Sonya — survived a late-term abortion, disability, adoption

**Beginning:** Sonya was born where no one expected to meet her alive — doctors were preparing to deliver a stillborn child after a late-term abortion, but she took her first breath on her own. Her first months passed through a string of shelters, until a woman named Tamara took her in — not wealthy, but firm in her faith.

**Choice 1:** At fourteen, Sonya overheard the adults talking about her in the kitchen: 'she was supposed to die.' That night she understood: her whole life, scars and limp included, had begun with someone else's decision — one she never got to make.

- (+10) Accept it as part of God's plan and start talking about it openly
- (-10) Bury it deep inside and never tell anyone

**Branch A:** Sonya started talking about her birth — first in her church community, then at youth gatherings. Every time was terrifying, but each time got a little easier. A few years later, her biological mother found her — lost, aged, struggling to find the words.

**Branch B:** Sonya learned to smile in a way that kept people from asking questions. She avoided talk of her childhood, of the scar on her leg. Years later, when her biological mother found her, Sonya didn't feel relief — she felt panic, as if her secret had finally been found out.

**Choice 2:** Her mother stands before her and quietly says, 'Hello — I'm your mother.' She has to decide, right now, what to do with those words.

- (+15) Forgive and let go, however hard that is
- (-15) Turn away and leave, unwilling to hear any of it

**Light ending:** Sonya forgave — not because it was easy, but because she decided she wouldn't carry someone else's guilt as her own for the rest of her life. Today she visits shelters and tells teenagers like herself that their life is not a mistake. The end of Sonya's story.

**Dark ending:** Sonya walked away that day and never looked back. Years later she still flinches at the word 'mother' and keeps everyone at a safe — and lonely — distance. The end of Sonya's story.

## Claire — survived a genocide/massacre, forgave her family's killers

**Beginning:** Claire grew up in a small town where everything felt simple and close-knit: Sunday church, loud family dinners, a father who knew every neighbor by name. When killings along ethnic lines swept the country, all of that vanished in a week. Neighbors hid Claire and seven other women in a cramped closet behind a wardrobe — a space once used for bedding now held eight bodies, so long as no one breathed too deeply or moved.

**Development:** Claire spent ninety-one days in that closet, hearing through the wall the voices of those hunting her and women like her — sometimes right nearby, once literally on the other side of the wardrobe. The women learned not to make a sound even in their sleep. Over those three months Claire grew so thin her clothes hung off her like a hanger, and instead of counting days she repeated to herself the names of relatives found dead one by one, in scraps of news.

**Choice 1:** In the third week of hiding, Claire caught herself unable to say the words of the prayer 'forgive us as we forgive,' which she'd known by heart since childhood — there was nothing inside her but hatred for the people killing outside, and shame for that hatred.

- (+10) Keep praying, even feeling nothing
- (-10) Stop praying and hold on to hatred alone

**Branch A:** Claire repeated the prayer every day, even when it felt like empty sound instead of prayer. Reading a scrap of Gospel that had ended up in the closet by candlelight, she suddenly understood: forgiveness isn't a feeling that arrives on its own, but a decision you have to make again every morning. When the massacre ended and the women finally stepped into the light, Claire had almost no family left — just an empty house and a list of names no one was left to carry.

**Branch B:** Hatred kept Claire on her feet when everything else — hunger, fear, despair — should have broken her. When the massacre ended, she had almost no family left, and inside her remained only that cold, hard feeling she mistook for strength.

**Choice 2:** Two years later, Claire runs into a man at the market whom she's certain killed people in her neighborhood — she recognizes him by the scar over his eyebrow, burned into her memory through a crack in the wardrobe. He recognizes her too and freezes on the spot, a basket of vegetables in his hands, as if it were an ordinary day at an ordinary market.

- (+15) Walk up and tell him she forgives him
- (-15) Walk past, wishing he'd pay for it too someday

**Light ending:** Claire walked up and spoke the words of forgiveness, though her throat tightened with every word and her hands shook so much she clasped them behind her back. It didn't bring her family back or erase the ninety-one days in the dark, but it took away hatred's power over her own life. Today she tells this story around the world — not to be pitied, but so someone else might find the same thing in themselves. The end of Claire's story.

**Dark ending:** Claire walked past without a word, clenching in her fist the same hatred she'd carried all those years. She survived in body, but sometimes, waking in the middle of the night, she still hears voices behind the wardrobe — and knows she never fully left that closet. The end of Claire's story.

## Rachel — abused by someone she trusted; justice and forgiveness

**Beginning:** From the age of thirteen, Rachel trained under a specialist the whole family and the whole club trusted — a man with a reputation, awards on his office wall, and a line of parents hoping to enroll their kids with him. Under the guise of treatment, he hurt her across many sessions, and she stayed silent, certain no one would believe her over him.

**Development:** Years later Rachel had retired from competitive sport, married, had children — from the outside, an ordinary, happy life. But at night the same office lighting haunted her dreams, and she noticed she couldn't watch broadcasts of the sport she once played. She told only her husband, in a whisper, years after their wedding — and even then, not everything.

**Choice 1:** Sixteen years had passed. Scrolling the news late one night while her husband slept, Rachel came across an article about other girls who'd survived the same thing from the same man — almost word for word the details she'd never told anyone in full. She realized: stay silent forever, or be the first to say it out loud under her own name.

- (+10) Come forward publicly, even if it wrecks her standing with the community
- (-10) Stay silent and keep living as if nothing happened

**Branch A:** Rachel filed the first report, under her real name, knowing there'd be no going back once it was public. Part of the community turned away from her, calling it a betrayal of a man they so respected. But she kept preparing her court statement in the evenings after the kids fell asleep, telling herself that justice and forgiveness weren't mutually exclusive.

**Branch B:** Rachel went on living as before, smiling at community gatherings while hiding a weight inside that only grew heavier with the years. She saw him on the news collecting yet another award, and each time felt like she was suffocating in her own living room.

**Choice 2:** At the trial, she is finally given the floor after all those years of silence. The whole room — journalists, former teammates, the defendant himself — watches her, waiting for what she will say.

- (+15) Speak both of justice and of forgiving him before God
- (-15) Say only that she wants him to suffer the way she suffered

**Light ending:** Rachel said words that surprised the whole room: she demanded the full weight of the law — and, in the same breath, looking him in the eye, said she forgave him before God. It didn't ease the pain instantly or return the stolen years, but it took from the past the right to decide who she'd become next. The end of Rachel's story.

**Dark ending:** Rachel got the sentence she'd fought for, but the fury she walked out of the courtroom with never let go of her. Years later she still wakes at night with the same feeling she had on the day of the verdict — victory turned out far lonelier than she'd imagined. The end of Rachel's story.

## Marcus — gang leader turned preacher

**Beginning:** By sixteen, Marcus ran one of the most dangerous gangs in the district — people crossed the street when they spotted him coming, and that feeling of power was the only thing that drowned out the memory of the home he'd run from at twelve. The only law he recognized was force, and the only family he had was whoever wore the same tattoos.

**Development:** Marcus had long since lost count of the fights he had been in and the people he had put in the hospital. At night, once the gang scattered, all that was left was an empty yard and a silence with no one left to prove himself to — and in that silence, sometimes, real fear crept in, though Marcus would never have admitted it out loud.

**Choice 1:** One day a stranger showed up preaching in the neighborhood — no bodyguards, no fear, as if he had no idea where he'd wandered into — and told Marcus straight to his face that God loved him and would not stop loving him no matter what he did. Marcus answered by hitting him and promising to kill him if he ever came back.

- (+10) Go anyway and hear what this preacher has to say
- (-10) Round up the gang and break up his next meeting

**Branch A:** Marcus showed up at the next meeting with half the gang — just to show who ran things, and maybe shut it down with one word. But listening to the sermon among strangers, he suddenly felt not anger but guilt for everything he had done — for the first time in years. His feet carried him forward, toward the stage, even as his mind screamed at him to turn around.

**Branch B:** Marcus and his crew came to break up the meeting, but the crush and shouting inside the packed hall scattered the gang in every direction before he could do anything. Marcus found himself alone, pressed into a crowd of strangers listening to the very preacher he had sworn to kill.

**Choice 2:** The speaker asks if anyone wants to give their life to God right now, without waiting for tomorrow. Marcus stands there, feeling something far bigger than any street fight he had ever been in being decided.

- (+15) Step forward and change everything
- (-15) Turn around and walk back to the street

**Light ending:** Marcus stepped forward under the eyes of strangers. That night one life ended and another began — no knife in his belt, no gang at his back, but also without the loneliness that had always hidden behind the power. Years later he goes out to street kids just like he once was and tells them what someone once told him. The end of Marcus's story.

**Dark ending:** Marcus turned around and went back to the street, to the same yard and the same silence that scared him more than any gang. A few years later his name turned up in the news again — no longer as a leader's name, just another line in the district crime report, without a word about who he could have become. The end of Marcus's story.

## Michael — captivity and torture, PTSD and alcoholism, forgave his torturer

**Beginning:** Michael came home after more than a year in captivity, where he was tortured — his body healed faster than his mind. Waiting for him was a wife who no longer recognized the man who had come back in place of the one she had seen off to serve — and nightmares that did not let go of a single night.

**Development:** By day Michael seemed almost like his old self — joking, going to work, smiling at neighbors. But the moment the lights went out, he was back in the camp: the same guard's voice, the same smell, the same helplessness. He started fearing the dark more than he had feared it in captivity itself, and admitted it to no one, including himself.

**Choice 1:** Michael started drinking just to get a little sleep without dreams of the camp, and every month the dose he needed grew. One night he woke up with his hands around his own wife's throat — he had dreamed he was strangling the guard he privately called Bird. In tears, his wife silently packed her things to leave, without a word of reproach — which was more frightening than any scream.

- (+0) Beg her to stay and promise to quit drinking
- (-5) Let her go without a word, thinking he deserves to be alone

**Common branch:** His wife went to meet a visiting preacher anyway, more out of despair than faith — and came home different: calm, almost glowing, as if she had found what she had been searching for for years. She asked nothing of Michael, just invited him to come with her next time, if he wanted.

- (+10) Go with her and listen
- (-10) Stay home drinking alone

**Choice 2:** The preacher speaks of war, suffering, and how God asks not for a man's strength but for his faith — simple words that somehow break through what years of therapy never touched. Michael suddenly remembers every promise he made to God in the camp, back when he thought he would not live to see morning.

- (+15) Pray honestly for the first time in years and forgive his torturer
- (-15) Get up and walk out silently, not ready to forgive

**Light ending:** That same night, for the first time in years, Michael had no nightmares — no guard's voice, no smell, no helplessness. He wrote a letter to the man he had called Bird — not to excuse him, but to finally free himself of a weight he had carried longer than the captivity itself had lasted. His wife stayed, and the nightmares never came back. The end of Michael's story.

**Dark ending:** The nightmares never let Michael go. In time there was no wife left beside him, no strength left to fight them alone, and the guard's voice grew quieter — simply because Michael had stopped fighting at all. The end of Michael's story.

## Anthony — corruption and prison, converted through a book, ministry to former inmates

**Beginning:** Anthony was the right hand of a powerful man — the kind who solves problems without asking unnecessary questions — and took pride in how well he did it. He had long since stopped asking himself where the job ended and the crime began, because that question would have kept him up at night if he ever asked it seriously.

**Development:** At the peak of his career, Anthony had an office overlooking the whole city, the respect of people he once feared, and total confidence he would never be caught — because he was too careful, and too useful to the people who mattered. That confidence collapsed on an ordinary Tuesday, with one phone call from a lawyer.

**Choice 1:** The scheme Anthony was part of unraveled faster than he could put together a defense. Journalists were already printing his name on front pages, the investigation dug deeper by the day, and former partners gave testimony against him one after another to save themselves at the cost of his freedom.

- (+10) Admit guilt and take responsibility
- (-10) Pin it all on subordinates and hold out to the last

**Branch A:** Anthony went to the investigator himself and admitted to part of the charges that were, in fact, true — a decision his lawyer called suicidal. It cost him his reputation and part of his sentence for conspiracy, but for the first time in years it lifted a weight he had carried for months without admitting it even to himself.

**Branch B:** Anthony held out to the end, shifting blame onto those below him in the chain and hiring ever more expensive lawyers. The sentence caught up with him anyway — only now with the added mark of a man who sold out his own people to save himself, and did not even save himself.

**Choice 2:** In prison, in a cell built for four, a worn book about faith falls into Anthony's hands, left behind by a cellmate who had just been released. The first pages seem dull and naive — exactly what he would expect from a book like that.

- (+15) Read the book to the end and let himself change
- (-15) Toss the book aside and count down the days to release

**Light ending:** Anthony finished the book in one sleepless night — and did not recognize his former self in its final chapter. When he got out, he did not go back to power; instead he started visiting prisons, now from the other side of the bars, to talk to men just like he used to be — and it turned out to be far harder, and far more important, than any deal he had ever made. The end of Anthony's story.

**Dark ending:** Anthony served his sentence to the day and walked free the same man who had walked in — maybe a little more careful. A couple of years later his name surfaced in the news again, this time in a new case, and the book stayed unread on a shelf in a rented apartment. The end of Anthony's story.

## Christine — lecturer and activist, converted through a years-long friendship with a pastor

**Beginning:** Christine taught at a university and was a well-known activist — for years she had written articles against everything she called religious obscurantism, and took pride in a reputation as someone impossible to out-argue. She never expected a letter from an obscure local pastor, disagreeing with one of her columns, to upend everything she believed.

**Development:** Christine wrote the pastor a scathing reply, expecting either silence or a counter-sermon she could easily tear apart in her next column. Instead came a simple dinner invitation — no argument attached, no attempt to change her mind, which struck her as either foolish or something she did not understand.

**Choice 1:** The pastor never argued with her in person — he and his wife simply set the table and listened to her all evening, without interrupting or judging. Christine holds a second dinner invitation in her hands and has to decide what to make of it — curiosity, or some politeness she cannot quite explain.

- (+10) Go to dinner out of curiosity, strange as it is
- (-10) Throw out the letter and write a scathing article about it

**Branch A:** The dinners became regular, every few weeks. The pastor and his wife never once tried to out-argue her — they just listened and stayed close, even when Christine deliberately provoked a fight. Out of stubbornness more than anything, hoping to find contradictions in the Bible for a future article, she started reading it herself, carefully, cover to cover.

**Branch B:** The article came out scathing and made the rounds among her colleagues, gathering plenty of approving replies. But something kept Christine up at night — the politeness of these people she had so carefully mocked in print did not fit her own theory of who they were supposed to be.

**Choice 2:** Two years into steady, careful reading, Christine catches herself mid-lecture, unable to honestly defend her old views to her students anymore. Accepting the new ones means losing almost everything: her career, her circle of friends, her reputation, the article she once took pride in.

- (+15) Accept the faith, even knowing the cost
- (-15) Set the Bible aside and go back to her old life

**Light ending:** Christine accepted the faith — and, as she had feared, lost almost everything she had spent years building: her position, some of her friends, her public image as an uncompromising fighter. But she calls it not a collapse, but an honest ruin, after which it finally became possible to build something real. The end of Christine's story.

**Dark ending:** Christine set the Bible back on the shelf and returned to her old life, her old columns, her old certainty in herself. But she still sometimes writes the pastor and his wife short letters — she just cannot explain, even to herself, why she cannot fully break away from them. The end of Christine's story.

## Paige — suicide attempt, disability, became a pro-life advocate

**Beginning:** At sixteen, Paige decided she no longer wanted to live — not on impulse, but after months of quiet, methodical despair that none of her family suspected. She survived, but was left in a wheelchair: her body, the one thing that had always obeyed her, failed even this last attempt.

**Development:** In the first weeks at the hospital, Paige barely spoke — not to the doctors, not to her parents, who came every day with the same helpless faces. She stared at the ceiling and waited for the pain to pass, both the physical kind and the other kind that had brought her here, but neither went anywhere.

**Choice 1:** Lying motionless for weeks in the hospital, Paige hears her roommate — an ordinary older woman recovering from surgery — quietly praying aloud every night before sleep, not for show, just out of a lifetime's habit. One day she is invited to join, with no pressure, the way you would offer someone water.

- (+10) Listen, and one day try praying herself
- (-10) Ignore it and grow even angrier at the world

**Branch A:** Paige began praying clumsily, not knowing the right words, just voicing her pain however it came out. For the first time in a long while, something inside grew a little quieter, though her body never obeyed as it once had, and the wheelchair stayed part of her life.

**Branch B:** Paige grew angry at her roommate, at the doctors, at her own body for refusing to die the way she wanted and planned. The anger never left — it only grew, day by day, inside four identical hospital walls.

**Choice 2:** A year after being discharged, already used to the wheelchair, Paige is asked to speak to schoolchildren in her hometown and tell her story — openly, in front of everyone, including what she tried to do to herself and why.

- (+15) Agree, and tell it all exactly as it happened
- (-15) Refuse — let no one know

**Light ending:** Paige rolled out onto the stage in her wheelchair and told it all without polish. After the talk, a girl from the audience came up to her, shaking, and said she had thought about the same thing just last week — and changed her mind after hearing Paige's story. The end of Paige's story.

**Dark ending:** Paige refused to speak and went on living, hiding her scars under her clothes and her story under familiar silence. She never found out how many people she might have helped with a few honest words about that night. The end of Paige's story.

## Michelle — kidnapped and enslaved by traffickers, faith as an anchor

**Beginning:** Michelle was kidnapped at twenty-one and held in trafficking for almost twenty years — beatings, being sold from hand to hand, several desperate escapes and just as many recaptures. She held onto one simple thought, repeating it like a prayer: that one day she would see her children again, the ones left at home the night she was taken.

**Development:** Michelle lost count of the cities and the owners she passed through — the names kept changing, but the fear stayed exactly the same. The one thing that never changed was a photo of her children, which she kept sewn into the lining of the one piece of clothing she had left, unfolding it only in the dark, when no one could see.

**Choice 1:** After yet another brutal punishment for trying to contact home, Michelle is left alone in the dark of a shed and remembers a prayer her grandmother taught her as a child — simple words that had long felt like they belonged to another, now-impossible life.

- (+10) Whisper the prayer, even without believing anyone will hear
- (-10) Decide there is no God, since He allows this

**Branch A:** Michelle began repeating that prayer every night — not because she believed, but because she had nothing else left besides the words and the photo in the lining. Slowly the prayer became the one place where she felt like a person with a name, not merchandise.

**Branch B:** Michelle stopped praying and held on only to anger and the thought of her children, like the last fuel she had left. The anger gave her strength to keep moving and surviving, but it burned away everything inside her that was not pure survival — compassion, tears, even the ability to feel anything besides rage.

**Choice 2:** After her release — almost accidental, the result of a raid she only learned about once she had already been led outside — a social worker, herself a deeply religious woman, offers to take Michelle to church with her. Just to try it, no one forcing her, nothing expected in return.

- (+15) Go, and let herself trust again
- (-15) Refuse — trust cost too much last time

**Light ending:** Michelle went. The faith that began as a whisper in the dark of a shed became the foundation of her new life in freedom — today she helps find and rescue others, the way she was once rescued, and holds her now-grown children in her arms again. The end of Michelle's story.

**Dark ending:** Michelle refused and built a new life alone, never fully trusting anyone again, not even her own grown children. She is free in body, but at night she still locks not just the apartment door, but every lock she can find. The end of Michelle's story.

## Amber — addiction and life on the street, recovery through a shelter

**Beginning:** For years Amber lived between drugs, alcohol, and the street, selling herself for her next fix — a path that started with one party at sixteen and quietly became the only life she remembered. She had long since stopped believing any other life was possible for her.

**Development:** On winter nights Amber sometimes thought about how much time she had left — not with horror, but with the tired indifference of someone who had long since stopped making plans past the next hit. The only thing that still recalled another possible life was an old photo of herself in her final year of school, which she still carried, for some reason, in her jacket pocket.

**Choice 1:** A woman from a street mission approaches Amber, not for the first time, with hot coffee and not a single word of judgment — just talking to her like a person, every week, despite Amber having been rude to her before.

- (+10) Agree to come to the mission shelter for even one night
- (-10) Wave her off — she's heard promises that meant nothing before

**Branch A:** Amber agreed to come to the mission shelter for one night and stayed a month. At the shelter, for the first time in a long while, nothing was asked of her in return — only a bed, a plate of food, and time to start over, if she wanted it.

**Branch B:** Amber waved her off and went back to her usual streets that same night. A couple of weeks later, the same woman found her again on the same corner, with the same hot coffee, without a single reproach for the refusal.

**Choice 2:** Having gotten through the first hard weeks of sobriety, withdrawal, and sleepless nights, Amber faces a choice: go to a group meeting where she will have to tell her story out loud to strangers, or go back to where no one asks questions or judges.

- (+15) Go, and tell it exactly as it happened
- (-15) Go back to the street — at least there's no need to explain anything

**Light ending:** Amber told her story in a circle of women just like her, and for the first time in years felt no shame — only enormous relief. Today she brews that same hot coffee for other women on the same streets where she once stood herself. The end of Amber's story.

**Dark ending:** Amber went back to the street that same night, as always. The woman with the coffee still looks for her every week, so far without luck, and the old photo from her jacket pocket has long since been lost. The end of Amber's story.

## Leah — conversion from a strict religious family, risking her life

- (+10) Tell the truth, whatever it costs
- (-10) Lie and keep her faith completely secret

**Branch B:** Leah lied and kept living two lives — at home, submissive and unchanged; at church on Sundays, under a different name, someone else entirely, free for a few hours a week. The lie grew heavier with every week, eating away at her from the inside faster than she could notice.

**Light ending:** Leah left that same night with one bag and no goodbyes. **Dark ending:** Leah stayed and locked her faith deep inside, somewhere no one could find it in a room search. She is alive and formally forgiven by her family, but she says part of her died that night along with the decision to stay. The end of Leah's story.

## Nathan — atheist journalist, converted through his own investigation

**Beginning:** Nathan was an atheist journalist who took pride in never believing a single fact without proof — that skeptic's reputation mattered to him more than almost any relationship. When his wife suddenly came to faith after several hard years of marriage, he was absolutely certain he could easily prove to her with facts that she was wrong.

**Development:** Nathan started with what he considered an easy warm-up — a quick rundown of the objections to the resurrection he'd heard dozens of times at press gatherings. But every objection he actually checked, instead of just repeating from memory, fell apart at the first serious source, and that irritated him far more than he was willing to admit.

**Choice 1:** Nathan decides to use every journalistic skill he has to methodically, genuinely demolish his wife's faith with evidence, rather than just wave it off over dinner.

- (+10) Start a genuinely honest investigation, wherever it leads
- (-10) Only gather facts that confirm what he already believes

**Branch A:** For two years Nathan methodically studied historical documents, interviewed experts, historians, and lawyers, trying to find even one weak point in the resurrection account — and the deeper he dug, professionally, the harder it became to refute it by the standards of his own profession.

**Branch B:** Nathan put together a folder of objections that satisfied him over breakfast, but fell apart at the first serious conversation with people who had actually studied the question for years, rather than just repeating common talking points.

**Choice 2:** After two years of an investigation that started as an attack on his wife's faith, Nathan sits with a conclusion completely at odds with where he began two years earlier — and that discovery frightens him more than any story he's ever investigated.

- (+15) Accept the conclusions of his own investigation and embrace the faith
- (-15) Discard his own conclusions, because changing his mind is too uncomfortable

**Light ending:** Nathan admitted: staying an atheist after an investigation like that would take more blind faith than becoming a Christian — and his professional integrity could not allow that anymore. He left journalism to spend his time explaining to others the very investigation that had turned his own life upside down. The end of Nathan's story.

**Dark ending:** Nathan threw the folder with his own conclusions into a desk drawer and kept his old public opinion — just to avoid admitting to his colleagues that he had been wrong for two years straight. His wife never brought up the subject at dinner again. The end of Nathan's story.

## Ethan — from a religious family, converted through a years-long argument with a friend

**Beginning:** Ethan had known the sacred texts by heart since childhood and had spent his whole life preparing to defend his faith — that is what his whole family expected, proud of what a serious child he had grown into. At university he became friends with a Christian, and their friendship quickly turned into a years-long, but always respectful, argument.

**Development:** The argument went on for years over tea after lectures, never once turning into a quarrel — they respected each other too much to get personal. Ethan knew all the standard answers to the standard questions in advance, but his friend stubbornly refused to ask standard questions, digging far deeper than Ethan was used to answering.

**Choice 1:** After one such conversation, his friend raised a serious historical argument from primary sources that Ethan — for all his preparation — could not find a convincing answer to, and that frightened him far more than any argument before it.

- (+10) See the question through to the end, even if he will not like the answer
- (-10) Close the subject and never raise it with his friend again

**Branch A:** For three and a half years Ethan studied the sources of both faiths in the evenings, secretly from most people he knew — not to win the next round of tea-time debate, but to finally, once and for all, learn the truth, however uncomfortable it turned out to be.

**Branch B:** Ethan avoided the subject for months, finding ever new excuses to postpone meeting his friend. But the question left unanswered kept sounding in his head every time he opened his usual books before bed.

**Choice 2:** Years into secret study, Ethan arrives at a conclusion that frightens him: he can no longer honestly defend his old faith to himself, let alone to others — and an open conversion will cost him nearly every relationship he has, including his family.

- (+15) Accept what he believes to be true, whatever the cost
- (-15) Stay in his old faith for the sake of family and friends

**Light ending:** Ethan made the choice he calls the most painful of his life — and, as he feared, lost nearly everyone he loved within a single year. He spent the time that remained honestly explaining to others the path of doubt and loss he had walked himself. The end of Ethan's story.

**Dark ending:** Ethan stayed in his old faith, keeping his family and familiar circle of friends, but his friend's question was never honestly answered — just buried deep enough not to keep him up at night. The end of Ethan's story.
